How to attempt a 100-mark paper in 3 hours

CA Inter Group I · 7 min read · 2–5 PM, reading from 1:45 PM

100 marks, 3 hours. That means 1.8 minutes per mark. Keeping this number in mind is the most important thing — because in the exam you don't realise how time is passing, and in the last 30 minutes you find out that 30 marks of paper are still left. This article has the full plan: from the reading time to the last minute.

1:45 – 2:00 PM — Reading time (15 minutes, free)

These 15 minutes are not for writing, they are for reading and planning. Use them like this:

  • Read the whole paper once — which questions are easy, which are difficult, which are from your strong chapters.
  • Decide the order of attempt. Start with the questions you know best — this builds confidence and secures marks.
  • Write the time against every question — like "Q1: 25 min", "Q2: 20 min". This is your time budget.
  • Identify the compulsory question (if there is one) — it must not be left out.

Writing is not allowed in reading time, but thinking is. Don't waste this time — these 15 minutes make the plan for your whole attempt.

2:00 – 4:45 PM — Writing time (165 minutes)

Now start writing. Budget your time like this:

WorkTimeNote
Strong questions first~90 minDo what you know first — secure the marks.
Medium questions~50 minWhat you know but takes time — in the second round.
Difficult / remaining~25 minAttempt even what you don't know — write something, don't leave it blank.

Time limit for every question

  • 1 mark = 1.8 minutes. A 20-mark question = not more than 36 minutes. A 10-mark question = 18 minutes.
  • The 2-minute rule: if you are stuck on a question for more than 2 minutes (nothing is coming to mind), leave it and move to the next one. You can come back and try again — getting stuck in the first attempt is wasting time.
  • Attempt every question. Leaving it blank gives zero; writing something can get you step marks. Write the working notes, the format, or even the first step.

4:45 – 5:00 PM — The last 15 minutes (revision)

These 15 minutes are not for a new question. In them:

  • Complete the questions that are left incomplete (working notes, totals, conclusions).
  • Check the totalling — totalling errors are common in Tax and Accounts.
  • Check the question numbers — make sure you didn't write a wrong number.
  • Check your roll number and details on the first page of the answer sheet.

Presentation — it also earns marks

  • Write headings and sub-headings — it makes your answer easier for the examiner to understand.
  • Write working notes separately, with "Working Note: 1" written on them — this tells the examiner where the calculation is.
  • Underline important figures — the final answer, total income, tax liability.
  • Keep your handwriting clean — if the examiner struggles to read it, marks can be cut even if the answer is right.

What should be the target? Passing is 40% in each paper and 50% aggregate in the group. That means you don't need 100 out of 100 — a solid 50–60 in every paper is enough. An 80 in one paper and a 30 in another will not work. So take every paper equally seriously, and don't forget one paper while chasing another.

Practise in mocks — not in the exam

This strategy will not come from reading alone, it comes from practice. Follow this same timetable in mock tests — 1:45 reading, 2:00 start, 4:45 revision. After 2–3 mocks you will know your own pace: how much time each type of question takes, and where time is saved. Don't try this strategy for the first time in the exam hall — try it in mocks, execute it in the exam.

The biggest mistake: spending 40 minutes on one difficult question and having no time left for the easy ones. In the exam, every question carries equal marks — a 20-mark difficult question and a 20-mark easy question are both worth 20 marks. Do the easy ones first, the difficult ones later.
