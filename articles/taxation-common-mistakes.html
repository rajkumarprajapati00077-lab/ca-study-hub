Taxation — common mistakes that cost you marks

CA Inter Group I · 8 min read · Income Tax + GST

In Taxation, it often happens that a student knows the answer and still loses marks. The reason is small mistakes — no working note written, a wrong section number, or not reading the question carefully. These 10 mistakes cost marks in every attempt. Fix them now.

1. Not writing working notes

This is the biggest mistake. In Tax, working notes earn marks — the examiner needs to see your calculation. If you write only the final answer and it is wrong, you get zero. If your working notes are correct and you slip in the last step, you still get step marks. Write a working note for every calculation — however small it is.

2. Reading only half the question

"Compute total income" and "compute tax liability" — these are two different questions. "Mr. X, a resident individual" and "Mr. X, a non-resident" — their answers are different. Underline the facts given in the first two lines of the question (residential status, age, nature of income). Read the question carefully in the 15-minute reading time — those 15 minutes are free, use them.

3. Getting the residential status wrong

Residential status is the first step in Income Tax — and if it goes wrong, the whole question goes wrong. Remember the basic conditions (182 days, and 60 days + 365 days in the last 4 years) and the additional conditions in a table. In every question, first decide: resident, non-resident, or resident but not ordinarily resident? Only then move ahead.

4. Mixing up the heads of income

Salary, house property, business, capital gains, other sources — every head has its own rules. Common mistakes:

  • In house property, calculating the annual value wrong (gross annual value, municipal taxes, 30% standard deduction).
  • In salary, forgetting the treatment of perquisites and allowances.
  • In capital gains, confusing when indexation applies and when it does not.
  • In business income, forgetting to add back disallowed expenses.

Make a one-page summary for every head — "what is included in this head, what is deducted, what is exempt."

5. Forgetting deductions (Chapter VI-A)

Deductions come after gross total income — Sec 80C (up to Rs. 1.5 lakh), Sec 80D (medical insurance), and other common deductions. The mistake is that a student calculates gross total income and jumps straight to tax, forgetting to apply deductions. Make a checklist: "Gross total income → minus deductions (80C, 80D, etc.) → total income → tax." Follow this checklist in every question.

6. Confusing place of supply in GST

In GST, the most marks are lost on place of supply and time of supply. Remember the basic rules:

  • Goods: the place of supply is generally where delivery takes place.
  • Services: the receiver's location (in B2B) or the supplier's location (in B2C, with some exceptions).
  • Inter-state vs intra-state: the place of supply decides whether IGST or CGST + SGST applies.

Put the place of supply rules in a flow chart — "Is it goods or a service? Is the receiver registered? What is the location?" — and follow that flow chart in every question.

7. Applying ITC rules wrongly

Input Tax Credit has blocked credit rules (like motor vehicles, food, personal use) and time-limit rules. The common mistake is claiming ITC on every input. Remember the blocked credit list separately — it is a short list, but a 4–5 mark question can be made from it.

8. Remembering old rates and limits

Tax rates and limits (like the Rs. 1.5 lakh limit of 80C) do not stay the same every year. Remember only the rates and limits that apply to the Jan 2027 attempt. Write "Jan 2027" next to every rate in your notes — so that an old rate coming to mind does not confuse you.

9. Calculation slips (totalling errors)

Tax questions are long — you have to add 15–20 figures. One totalling error spoils the whole answer. To avoid it:

  • Write the figures in one column, straight and clear.
  • After totalling, add them once again — it takes 30 seconds, but it saves 5 marks.
  • Trust the calculator; don't try to add in your head.

10. Taking MCQ / case-study questions lightly

The Tax paper also has MCQs (case-study based). People think they are "easy" and leave them for the end, then run out of time. MCQs also need practice — solve the MCQs from RTP and MTP, and keep the habit of working notes in them too.

One habit that changes everything: before solving any Tax question, take 30 seconds and write down 3 things — (1) what is to be calculated (total income / tax liability / GST?), (2) which head or section applies, (3) what deductions or exemptions exist. These 30 seconds will stop you from going in the wrong direction.
