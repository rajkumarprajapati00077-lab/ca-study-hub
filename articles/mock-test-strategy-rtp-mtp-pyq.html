Mock test strategy — how to use RTP / MTP / PYQ

CA Inter Group I · 8 min read · ICAI mock series, error log

RTP, MTP, PYQ — all three are ICAI's official material, but each has a different job. A student who uses all three the same way does not get the benefit of any of them. This article covers the correct use of each, how to take a mock, and the system for analysing it afterwards.

Each one has a different job

MaterialWhat it isWhen to use it
PYQ (Past Year Questions)Real papers from previous attemptsAt the start of revision — to understand the pattern
MTP (Mock Test Papers)ICAI's mock papers, exam-like difficultyIn the middle of revision — for exam simulation
RTP (Revision Test Papers)Revision material for the latest attempt, with amendments and trendsIn the last 3–4 weeks — to catch the latest things

PYQ — to understand the pattern

PYQ shows you what type of questions come in the exam, which chapters they come from most, and what the level of the questions is. Solve PYQ chapter-wise — for example, while studying Amalgamation, solve its PYQ questions. This shows you what kind of questions come from that chapter.

MTP — for exam simulation

Take MTP like a full paper — 3 hours, at exam time (2–5 PM), no phone, no break. The purpose of MTP is to feel the pressure of the exam hall and test your time management. More important than the marks in MTP is the time record — how much time each question took, where you got stuck.

RTP — for the latest trends

RTP has the latest amendments and the important topics for this attempt. Read RTP in the last 3–4 weeks — it shows what could be new in the exam this time. Solve the RTP questions, but don't take them like a mock — read them as study material.

How to take a mock — 5 rules

  1. Take it at exam time. 2–5 PM (or 10 AM–1 PM for ICAI mocks). This sets your body clock to exam time.
  2. Take it in exam conditions. Phone off, table clean, 3 hours without getting up. A half mock is of no use.
  3. Write on an answer sheet. Not in a rough notebook — in proper answer sheet format. This also practises your presentation.
  4. Note the time. Write down how much time each question took. This shows where time is wasted.
  5. Check it the same day. Checking a mock 2 days later is of no use — check it the same day or by the next morning, while the questions are still in your mind.

Analysis after the mock — the most important step

Taking a mock is easy, analysing it is hard — and the analysis is the real benefit. After every mock, do this:

  • Make an error log. In a notebook or sheet, write: which question went wrong, and why (concept not clear / calculation slip / no time left / question read wrongly). These 4 categories are enough.
  • Update your weak chapters list. A chapter where you made a mistake twice is a weak chapter — revise it again next week.
  • Analyse your time. Which question took too much time? Did you spend too long on a difficult question and leave an easy one?
  • Look at the trend in marks. First mock 35, second 42, third 48 — if the trend is going up, you are moving in the right direction. Don't decide anything from one mock's marks; look at the trend.

How many mocks should you take? In the last 60 days, take at least 3–4 full mocks per subject — ICAI's 2 series (6 papers) plus 2–3 more from MTP/PYQ. Taking fewer mocks with good analysis is better than taking many mocks with no analysis. 10 mocks without analysis are not equal to 3 mocks with good analysis.

The mistake everyone makes: getting demotivated by mock marks. Mock marks are not exam marks — a mock exists so that mistakes are found now, not in the exam. If you score 35 in a mock, that is a good thing — you found out where to work. Scoring 70 in a mock, feeling happy, and not analysing it is worse than scoring 35 and analysing it.
