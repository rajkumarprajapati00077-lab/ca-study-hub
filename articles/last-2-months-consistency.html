Consistency

How to stay consistent in the last 2 months

CA Inter Group I · 7 min read · Routine, discipline, burnout

In the last 2 months, most students fall behind not because they can't study, but because they can't stay consistent — they study 12 hours one day, then nothing for 3 days. Motivation lasts a day, routine lasts every day. This article has a practical way to build a routine — no lectures, only what works.

1. Make a fixed routine — and write it down

A routine should not live in your head, it should be on paper. Make a simple timetable:

  • Fixed waking and sleeping time. Wake up and sleep at the same time every day — when the body clock is set, studying in the morning becomes easier.
  • 3 study blocks. Morning, afternoon, evening — each block 2.5–3 hours. One subject in each block.
  • Write the breaks too. A 10-minute break after every 50 minutes, and meal times separately. A routine without breaks is incomplete.

Keep the routine realistic. It is better to make a 9-hour routine and study 9 hours than to make a 14-hour routine and study 6 hours. A routine is one that gets followed, not one that looks good on paper.

2. Phone discipline — the biggest enemy

Losing 1 hour to the phone is easy; getting that 1 hour back is hard. For this:

  • Keep the phone in another room during study time. Putting it on silent is not enough — even seeing the phone is a distraction.
  • Keep a fixed time for social media. 30 minutes a day, in the evening — that's it. Not more.
  • Use study apps. Set a 50-minute block with a timer app (Pomodoro) — when the timer is running, you feel less like touching the phone.

3. Plan for bad days in advance

In 60 days, there will be 5–6 days when you won't feel like studying — tiredness, low mood, or some problem. For those days, decide a minimum plan in advance:

  • A minimum of 2 hours. Even on a bad day, study 2 hours — only revision, no new chapter. 2 hours keeps the routine from breaking.
  • Only revise. Don't study anything new on a bad day — revise the old. This way you still make progress, and there is no pressure.
  • Don't blame yourself. One bad day spoils nothing. Come back to the routine the next day — that's it. Five bad days together are a problem; one bad day is not.

4. Avoiding burnout

Burnout happens when you study for a week without a break. To avoid it:

  • Keep half a day off every week. One afternoon or evening a week — do light revision, or nothing at all. The brain needs rest.
  • Sleep properly. 7 hours of sleep is necessary. Studying till 2 AM and waking up at 6 AM is not consistency, it is burnout.
  • Food and water. It sounds basic, but people forget it during exams. Eat properly, drink water — the brain needs fuel.
  • Exercise or a walk. A 20–30 minute walk or light exercise every day — it improves sleep and keeps the mind fresh.

5. Track your progress — every day

The easiest way to stay consistent is to see your progress. Every night, take 2 minutes and write:

  • How many hours did I study today?
  • What got completed (chapter / revision / mock)?
  • What do I have to do tomorrow?

This 2-minute task shows you every day that you are moving forward. And when progress is visible, motivation comes on its own — there is no need to look for motivation outside.

Remember: consistency does not mean studying 12 hours every day. Consistency means studying every day — whether it is 6 hours or 10 hours. In 60 days, a student who studies 8 hours every day will always be ahead of a student who studies 14 hours for 10 days and 0 hours for 10 days. Slow and steady wins.

The last 10 days — what changes?

  • Keep the study hours the same — don't suddenly try 14 hours, the body will get tired.
  • Don't start anything new. Only short notes, formula sheets and weak chapters.
  • Keep the exam things ready in advance. Admit card, pens, calculator, watch — check everything one day before the exam.
  • Take special care of sleep. Sleep early on the night before the exam — studying all night means the brain will not work in the exam.
